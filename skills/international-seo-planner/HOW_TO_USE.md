# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. Check the hreflang on our country and language versions, and tell me which market we should add next.

## What you need

- **Nothing connected, at minimum.** Paste your version URLs, a URL list, your XML sitemap or an hreflang export from any crawler, plus a Search Console country export if you have one.
- **Better: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected to Claude,** with Google Search Console, DataForSEO, the crawler and Bing Webmaster added. The skill then pulls impressions by country, local keyword volumes and every page's tags itself.
- **A few business facts:** what differs by country (prices, currency, legal text, products), which languages your support team speaks, and whether you have a local company anywhere.

## Example requests

**Hreflang check**
Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. Check the hreflang across our US, UK and German versions and give me the corrected tags.

**Wrong country ranking**
Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. UK searchers keep landing on our US pages. Find out why and how much traffic it affects.

**Next market**
Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. We're choosing between France and Spain. Size both markets in their own language and tell me which one to launch first.

**From an export**
Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. Here is our hreflang export as a CSV. Check every set and write the sitemap entries.

**New site setup**
Hey Claude, I just added the "international-seo-planner" skill. We're launching in Germany and Austria. Should we use a .de domain or a /de/ folder, and which hreflang codes do we need?

## What you get

1. **A market table:** impressions, clicks and position by country, which version serves each one, the local search volume in its own language, and a verdict: serve next, watch or skip for now.
2. **A URL setup recommendation:** subfolders, country domains or subdomains, and language-only or language-region codes, with the facts behind it.
3. **An hreflang check per page set:** return links, codes, self references, x-default, canonicals and status, with the issues ranked by the impressions at stake.
4. **A wrong-country table:** the share of each country's impressions that land on a version meant for another market, and why.
5. **Ready-to-paste output:** corrected hreflang tags, sitemap entries and a localized keyword list per market, mapped to pages.
6. **Rollout steps,** with a release check before any new page joins the hreflang set.

## What it can change for you

With your yes, after you have published the new sitemap file, it can submit it to Google Search Console and to Bing. It never edits your site: tags, canonicals and templates are written out for you or your developer.

## Tips

- Give it the same page in each version (home, pricing, a top feature page) if your translated URLs don't match by path.
- It writes the keyword list for a new language, not the translation. Hand that list to a native writer.
- Run it again 4 weeks after fixing the tags to see the wrong-country share fall.
