# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. Build a hiring kit for a mid-level Paid Search Manager, with a job description, interview questions, a paid take-home and a 30-60-90 plan.

## What you need

- **The role and level,** and what the hire must achieve in their first year, in your own words.
- **Your pay range and where it comes from.** The skill never makes up a salary; without one, the job description gets a marker to fill in before posting.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected to Claude,** with Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4 or Search Console added. The skill then pulls your real baselines for the scorecard and builds the take-home from your own data, with every name and number disguised. Without a connection it works from what you describe.
- **Your draft job description,** if you have one, so it can check the wording.

## Example requests

**PPC hire with live data**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. We're hiring a Paid Search Manager. Use our Google Ads account to set first-year targets and build a take-home from our search terms. Pay is $95,000 to $115,000 from our September comp review.

**SEO hire**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. Build a kit for a senior SEO specialist. Search Console is connected; the take-home should test how they pick pages to fix.

**Check a job description**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. Here's our digital marketing manager job description. Flag any biased or exclusionary wording and rewrite it.

**Interview questions only**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. Give me marketing interview questions for a head of marketing, with what a strong answer sounds like.

**A freelancer**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-hiring-kit" skill. We want a freelance paid social manager for Meta. Build a short paid project and a scorecard for the first three months.

## What you get

1. **A first-year scorecard:** 3 to 5 outcomes, each with a baseline from your data, a target and how it will be measured.
2. **A job description** ready to post, with your pay range.
3. **A wording check:** each biased or exclusionary phrase, why it's a problem, and a replacement.
4. **An interview loop:** stages, owners, questions, follow-ups, what strong and weak answers sound like, and a decision rule.
5. **A paid take-home:** the candidate brief, a disguised dataset, the masking log for you to approve, an answer key and a 100-point rubric.
6. **Reference-check questions** tied to the scorecard.
7. **A dated 30-60-90 plan** and review dates that reuse the same scorecard.

## What it can change for you

Nothing. It reads your data and writes documents; it never changes an ad account, a property or a site.

## Tips

- Settle the scorecard first. Everything else in the kit follows from it.
- Read the masking log before a take-home goes out; nothing leaves until you say yes.
- Score the take-home against the rubric before discussing it, so the first reader doesn't set the bar for everyone.
- At each review, rerun the same reports named in the scorecard so the numbers compare.
