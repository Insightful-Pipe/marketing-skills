# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "customer-advocacy-program" skill. Build our customer advocacy and reference program from this customer export, and tell me who to ask for what.

## What you need

- **Your customer export** (CSV, a Google Sheet or a paste): plan, start and renewal dates, NPS or health score, seats and active users, open escalations, do-not-contact flags and logo permission. Missing columns are listed as unknown, not guessed.
- **Your ask history** for the last 12 months, if you keep one, so nobody who just helped gets asked again.
- **Open deals** at proposal stage or later, by segment, if you want the plan sized to what sales needs.
- **Review counts** for you and two or three rivals on G2, Capterra or another software review site, with the date you read them.
- Nothing has to be connected. With the InsightfulPipe MCP, it can read the export from Google Sheets or plans and renewals from Stripe, check a rival's count in Google results through DataForSEO, and save the tracker and email templates after you confirm.

## Example requests

**Build the program**
Hey Claude, I just added the "customer-advocacy-program" skill. Here is our customer export and last year's reference calls. Build a customer reference program: who is ready, what to ask each one, and where sales has no references.

**Match a deal**
Hey Claude, I just added the "customer-advocacy-program" skill. A 40-seat logistics company switching from our main competitor wants a reference call this week. Who should we ask, and what should our account manager send them?

**Review push**
Hey Claude, I just added the "customer-advocacy-program" skill. How do we get more G2 reviews? We have 41 and our main rival has 127. Check how we ask today against G2's rules and plan the next six months.

**Review the quarter**
Hey Claude, I just added the "customer-advocacy-program" skill. Here is our advocacy tracker for the quarter. Who is overused, which segments still lack references, and which deals did our advocates take part in?

## What you get

1. **Stop items:** anything in how you ask today that breaks G2's rules or the FTC position, with the fix.
2. **Two pools:** customers ready to vouch for you privately, scored 0 to 100, and the review invitation list, built without looking at scores.
3. **The proof gap** by segment: references needed for open deals against references available now.
4. **One first ask per ready customer:** reference bench, case study, or quote and logo approval, with the points it costs.
5. **Deal matches** for a specific prospect, and who is resting until when.
6. **The review-site plan:** your gap to a named rival, the share of invitees who need to publish, whether that is realistic, and the incentive decision per site.
7. **Drafts** of every ask and the review invitation, and a tracker ready for a sheet.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact change, it can:
- put the tracker on a new Google Sheets tab
- save the ask emails as Klaviyo or Mailchimp templates

It never sends an email, contacts a customer or posts a review.

## Tips

- Keep one ask history for sales, CS and marketing. The budgets only work when every ask is logged.
- Run it each quarter, and save the review counts with their dates: the second run can measure each rival's pace.
- Let the account owner veto any ask. A customer in a hard renewal is not a reference this month.
