# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "ai-marketing-workflow-planner" skill. Here's our team's task list and last month's time log. Tell us which marketing tasks to automate with AI, which ones you should draft for us, what stays human, and how many hours it saves.

## What you need

- **Your recurring tasks:** a list, process notes or a calendar export, with who does each one and how often. Nothing else is required.
- **A time log, if you keep one:** pasted, or in a Google Sheet. Hours saved are counted from your own logged minutes; without a log, only the times you state are used.
- **Who approves what:** spend and ad account changes, public posts, customer emails. The skill never invents an approver; a missing one becomes an open question.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected.** Then every automation is checked against what is really connected, with read or Read & Write access, before the plan promises it.

## Example requests

**Team plan**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ai-marketing-workflow-planner" skill. We're a four-person marketing team. Here are our recurring tasks and our time log for October. Build our AI and automation plan with hours saved.

**One process**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ai-marketing-workflow-planner" skill. Our weekly paid media report takes Leo most of Monday morning. Can it run on its own? Write the build spec with the review step.

**Check what's possible**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ai-marketing-workflow-planner" skill. Check our connected accounts and tell me which of these tasks can really be automated today, and which need a connection or an access change first.

**AI policy**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ai-marketing-workflow-planner" skill. Write a short AI use policy for our marketing team: tools, data, brand, disclosure and who approves what.

## What you get

1. **A task table:** every recurring task scored on frequency, time, risk and data access, with the decision (automate, assist, keep human or leave as is) and the rule that made it.
2. **Hours saved a year,** counted from your own log and labelled as an estimate until the first four runs are measured.
3. **A connection check:** each platform the plan needs, its access, and what to connect or switch to read only.
4. **Build specs:** trigger, inputs, steps, output, review gate, what happens when it fails, owner, how to stop it and how to measure it.
5. **A one-page AI use policy** and a rollout in three waves.

## What it can change for you

With your yes, it can save the plan as a new tab in a Google Sheet you name. Nothing else: every post, email or ad account change in a spec is for your team to build and approve. Posts are planned as scheduler drafts and emails as drafts that a person sends.

## Tips

- Log minutes for two to four weeks before you plan. The median per task is what counts, so one bad day doesn't skew it.
- Start with the first wave only, and move a task on after four runs where the reviewer changed nothing that mattered.
- Run it again each quarter, with the new log, to see the real hours saved.
