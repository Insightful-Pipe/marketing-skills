# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "review-removal-handler" skill. We got a pile of 1-star Google reviews this month. Tell me which ones I can get removed and what to say on the rest.

## What you need

- **The reviews.** Paste each one with its site (Google, Yelp, Amazon or other), the star rating, the date and the full text, or share screenshots. Or connect Google Business Profile through the InsightfulPipe MCP, and the skill reads every review on your location itself.
- **Your records, if you can check them.** A job log, booking system or order list by date. "Not our customer" only counts when the records back it up.
- **Anything you already know** about a reviewer, such as a former employee or a competitor. The skill labels it as your statement.

Yelp and Amazon reviews are always pasted: there's no connector for them. Amazon doesn't let sellers reply to product reviews, so an Amazon review gets a report or keep verdict and no reply.

## Example requests

**A review attack**
Hey Claude, I just added the "review-removal-handler" skill. Five 1-star reviews showed up on our Google profile in two days and none of them are customers. Check whether it's a review bombing pattern and draft the reports.

**One review**
Hey Claude, I just added the "review-removal-handler" skill. Does this Google review break policy, and how do I report it? "I worked there for a year and they never paid overtime."

**Yelp and Amazon**
Hey Claude, I just added the "review-removal-handler" skill. Here are three Yelp reviews and two Amazon product reviews. Which ones go against the site's rules and can be reported? Draft replies for the Yelp ones I should answer, and tell me what the Amazon ones say I should fix.

**Follow up**
Hey Claude, I just added the "review-removal-handler" skill. Here's the tracker from last week. Check which reported reviews are gone and tell me which ones to appeal.

## What you get

1. **Patterns:** bursts of low ratings against your own usual rate, and reviews that repeat each other's text, with the numbers.
2. **A verdict for every review:** report it, report it with the pattern, hold until you check your records, or keep it and reply. Every report names the policy clause and quotes the words that break it.
3. **A report pack:** where to report each review and a short note to use.
4. **A reply for every Google and Yelp review,** marked "post now" or "hold". Each Amazon review you keep gets a short note on what it says to fix instead.
5. **A tracker** that, on the next run, shows which reported reviews are gone and which can be appealed.

## What it can do for you

With your yes on the exact text, it can post a reply on a Google review. It never posts anything on its own, and it can't file the report for you: Google, Yelp and Amazon only take reports through their own forms.

## Tips

- Report first, reply later. A reply to a review that gets removed is wasted.
- Don't report a review only because it's unfair. A harsh but real review stays, and a good reply does more than a failed report.
- Never offer a refund or a gift for removing or changing a review.
- Run it again a week after reporting, with the tracker, to see what came down.
