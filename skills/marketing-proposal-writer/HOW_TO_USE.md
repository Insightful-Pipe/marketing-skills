# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. Here are my discovery call notes and the audit for a new prospect. Write the proposal with three priced options from our rate card.

## What you need

- **Your source material:** discovery notes, a call transcript, a prospect audit or the RFP. Paste it or upload it. No connection is needed.
- **Your rate card:** an hourly rate per role or one blended rate, and the lowest effective hourly rate you'll accept. Without rates, you get hours and "hours x your rate" instead of fees.
- **The client's planned ad spend** per channel, the decision date and a kickoff date, if the notes don't say.
- **Optional:** once the client grants access, connect their Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4 and Search Console through the InsightfulPipe MCP and the skill pulls the counts and 90-day numbers itself. A rate card in Google Sheets can be read the same way.

## Example requests

**Proposal from a call**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. Turn this call transcript into a proposal for Google Ads and Meta Ads. Our blended rate is $120 an hour and our floor is $95.

**Marketing proposal template for a new service line**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. Write a proposal for local SEO for a 3-location clinic. Mark anything you need from me as a question instead of guessing.

**Statement of work**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. The client picked option B. Write the statement of work with deliverables, acceptance criteria and change control.

**RFP or tender**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. Here's a tender for digital marketing services. Build the compliance matrix, answer each section, and list the questions we should send before the deadline.

**Check my draft**
Hey Claude, I just added the "marketing-proposal-writer" skill. Check this proposal for scope traps and claims we can't back.

## What you get

1. **The problem in the client's words:** up to 3 quotes from the call, by role, each tied to a goal.
2. **Goals with baselines:** each goal has a number today, its source and window. A goal with no baseline gets "baseline first" (measured in days 1 to 14), not a target.
3. **Scope sized from the account:** hours per channel, with the campaign, ad group, keyword, ad set and content counts behind them.
4. **A dated 90-day plan** in three phases, with what the client must do and the gate for each phase.
5. **Two or three priced options:** monthly fee, fee as % of ad spend, setup fee and first-90-days total. Each is checked against your floor rate, and the effective hourly rate stays in your internal review.
6. **Projections as a range** from the account's own cost per result, never a guarantee.
7. **Assumptions, exclusions and change control,** plus a short cover email.
8. **An internal review you don't send:** the 13-point trap check, claims that need evidence, the pricing math and floor check, and the points to confirm with a lawyer.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact change, it can add a tab to a Google Sheet you name and write the options table into it. It never writes to the client's ad, analytics or Search Console accounts.

## Tips

- Paste your usual boilerplate. The trap check is most useful on the text you send every time.
- Give it your real time-tracking hours per channel if you have them; it uses those instead of its starting coefficients.
- The sample on this page is invented: a 4-location dental group and the agency pitching it. Paid enquiries (Google Ads booking requests plus Meta lead form submissions) come out at 196 to 252 a month on $18,000 of spend, so the "double your leads" line in the boilerplate gets flagged.
