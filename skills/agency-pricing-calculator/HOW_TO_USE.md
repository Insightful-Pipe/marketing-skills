# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "agency-pricing-calculator" skill. Work out what we should charge a new client for PPC management and social media management, and which pricing model to use.

## What you need

- **Your team's costs:** pay per role, on-costs, paid hours and how much of the time is billable. A freelancer gives one row: the salary they want, their hours and their tools.
- **Your annual overhead:** rent, software, insurance, sales and anything else not billed to one client.
- **A target margin.** If you have none, the skill shows prices at three margins so you can pick.
- **Hours per task,** from your time tracking if you have it. Without it, the skill starts from placeholders and asks you to confirm each one.
- **The client's scope:** platforms, spend, posts a month and reports. If the client's ad accounts are connected through the InsightfulPipe MCP, the skill counts campaigns, ad groups, ad sets, keywords, ads and spend itself. Otherwise paste the numbers from a prospect audit or an export, or describe the account.

## Example requests

**Social media management**
Hey Claude, I just added the "agency-pricing-calculator" skill. I'm a freelancer. How much should I charge for social media management: 20 posts a month on Instagram and LinkedIn, with community management? I want to earn 70,000 a year.

**PPC management pricing**
Hey Claude, I just added the "agency-pricing-calculator" skill. This prospect's Google Ads and Meta accounts are connected. Size the work from the accounts and price it as a retainer and as a percentage of spend. Which one should we use?

**Check our fee schedule**
Hey Claude, I just added the "agency-pricing-calculator" skill. We charge 15% of ad spend with a 1,500 minimum. Is that profitable on our small accounts, and at what spend does it start over-charging?

**Build a rate card**
Hey Claude, I just added the "agency-pricing-calculator" skill. Build our rate card for paid media, social media and SEO from our team costs, then write it to a new tab in our Google Sheet.

## What you get

1. **Loaded cost per billable hour** for each role, with the overhead share.
2. **The client's workload in hours,** line by line, with the account size behind each line.
3. **Four monthly pricing models side by side** (hourly, retainer, percentage of ad spend, performance) with the fee and margin of each at the client's real spend, plus the setup priced as a one-off project fee.
4. **The flip point:** the spend where a percentage fee stops under-charging, plus the zero-margin spend, below which it no longer covers cost.
5. **The minimum fee** that keeps your smallest account profitable, and how your current minimum compares.
6. **A rate card** with three sizes per service, an hourly rate for extra work and a setup fee.
7. **A client quote** with the recommended model, the hours it covers, the hours headroom before the fee only covers cost, and how much extra revenue the client needs to earn the fee back.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact values, it can write the rate card and quote to a new tab in a connected Google Sheet. It never edits an existing tab and never changes anything in the ad accounts.

## Tips

- Feed it real hours from your time tracking. The quote is only as good as the hours.
- Price the scope the client wants, not what they do today: the skill shows both.
- Rerun the flip point when a client adds campaigns or platforms. It holds for one scope only.
- See the sample: an invented five-role agency quoting an invented online furniture store, where its own percentage-of-spend schedule would have charged 27% less than the work costs at its target margin.
