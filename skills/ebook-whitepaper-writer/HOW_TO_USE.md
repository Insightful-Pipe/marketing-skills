# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "ebook-whitepaper-writer" skill. Turn our best blog posts about no-shows into an ebook for salon and clinic owners. Give me the outline and the first chapter.

## What you need

- **Nothing to connect, if you have a brief.** Paste or upload your brief, notes, drafts, articles and the sources you want cited.
- **For compile mode, the InsightfulPipe MCP with Search Console and GA4** (optional, but it's what picks the chapters). Claude reads which articles earn clicks and key events, which questions people search that your site doesn't answer yet, and how far readers really get in your long articles.
- **WordPress** (optional) to read your articles directly instead of pasting them, and to save the download page as a draft. **Google Sheets** (optional) if you keep a stats table there. **DataForSEO** (optional) for the questions people ask about a topic when you have no Search Console data.
- **Your answers to a few questions:** whitepaper or ebook, who it's for, the one thing the reader should do after the last page, and which of your own numbers may be published.

## Example requests

**Whitepaper from a brief**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ebook-whitepaper-writer" skill. Write a whitepaper for clinic managers on why online deposits cut no-shows. Here's my brief and three sources. The reader should book a demo at the end.

**Ebook from your best articles**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ebook-whitepaper-writer" skill. Build an ebook from our blog posts about email marketing. Use the posts that bring in the most sign-ups, and fill the gaps with what people search.

**Fill in a whitepaper template**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ebook-whitepaper-writer" skill. Here's our whitepaper template and my notes. Write it in full, and cut any number you can't source.

**Refresh an old guide**
Hey Claude, I just added the "ebook-whitepaper-writer" skill. Our 2022 guide is out of date. Check every figure, replace the ones a newer source covers, date or cut the rest, drop what's no longer true, and give the designer a new layout brief.

## What you get

1. **How the chapters were chosen:** in compile mode, each article's clicks and key events, which ones became chapters, and which were merged.
2. **Gap questions:** questions people search that your site doesn't answer well yet, each placed in a chapter.
3. **A chapter length set from your readers:** how many words they really get through on your long articles, and whether each chapter should put its takeaway up front.
4. **An evidence ledger:** every figure with its source and date, and why any figure was cut or replaced.
5. **The outline and the chapters in full,** with one pull quote per chapter.
6. **A page-by-page layout brief** your designer can build from.
7. **A short summary for the download page:** headline, abstract, three bullets and who it's for.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact page, it can save the download page summary as a **draft** page in WordPress. It never publishes, uploads files or edits an existing post.

## Tips

- Send every source you want cited up front. Figures without a source and a date stay out.
- Say which of your own numbers (customer data, survey results) may be published. Until you do, they're left out.
- Ask for the outline and first chapter first, approve the outline, then ask for the rest.
- Deciding which download to build, or whether to gate it, comes before this skill.
