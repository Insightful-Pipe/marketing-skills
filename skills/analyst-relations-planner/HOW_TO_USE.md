# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "analyst-relations-planner" skill. Build our analyst relations plan for the next 12 months and tell me how to get into the analyst report our buyers read.

## What you need

- **Your category in the words analysts use,** your product, and two to five competitors.
- **What you already know:** which firms your buyers mention in deals, any research contracts, past briefings and follow-ups you still owe, past placements, and the inclusion criteria from the last report if you have it.
- **Your proof:** customer counts, revenue, retention and usage figures with their dates, marked public, NDA only, or not to be shared. Roadmap items with dates.
- **A questionnaire,** if one has arrived, with its word limits.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP** with DataForSEO (to find which evaluations, editions and analysts show up in public news and blogs) and GA4 (for dated website numbers and visits from analyst and review sites). Without them, the skill works from what you paste.

Analyst reports sit behind paywalls and no connector reads them, so report contents and criteria always come from you.

## Example requests

**A full plan**
Hey Claude, I just added the "analyst-relations-planner" skill. We sell AP automation to mid-market finance teams. Map the analyst firms that cover us, score them, and plan our briefings for the next 12 months.

**Getting into one evaluation**
Hey Claude, I just added the "analyst-relations-planner" skill. We were left out of the last two editions of the evaluation our buyers ask about. Here are the inclusion criteria. Tell me what we're missing and when to brief them.

**A briefing deck**
Hey Claude, I just added the "analyst-relations-planner" skill. Outline a 30-minute analyst briefing deck from these figures, and drop anything we can't back up.

**A questionnaire**
Hey Claude, I just added the "analyst-relations-planner" skill. Draft answers to this analyst questionnaire within the word limits, and tell me which ones need input from the team.

## What you get

1. **Top actions,** starting with any inclusion criterion you don't meet yet.
2. **A coverage map:** each firm's evaluations and editions, the estimated cycle and next edition, competitors placed, named analysts, and a score that sets the firm's tier.
3. **A 12-month calendar** of briefings, inquiry calls (only where you have a contract), questionnaire windows and follow-ups.
4. **A proof ledger:** every number with its period, source and sharing label. Claims with no source are dropped.
5. **A briefing deck outline,** one slide per criterion, each slide tied to ledger proof.
6. **Questionnaire drafts,** each marked drafted, needs input or gap, with the word count.
7. **A tracking baseline:** monthly citations, visits from analyst and review sites, and placements by edition.

## What it never does

It never emails, books or messages an analyst, and it changes nothing in any account. Drafts are for you to review and send. It never invents a report, placement, analyst or criterion: what it can't find is marked unknown.

## Tips

- Paste the inclusion criteria if you have them. A criterion you don't meet matters more than any briefing.
- Confirm every analyst name on the firm's own site before anyone reaches out.
- Rerun it monthly to update the tracking counts, and review the plan each quarter.
