# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "market-entry-plan" skill. We've decided to enter Germany next. Plan how we get in, with a 90-day test budget and clear stop rules.

## What you need

- **The country you've chosen,** and the market you sell in today. The skill plans how to enter one market; it doesn't pick between countries.
- **Your plans and prices, gross margin, and how many sign-ups become paying customers.** Without the last one, every cost per customer is an estimate, and the skill says so.
- **The most you'll put into a 90-day test,** and the exchange rate you want to use.
- **What buyers from that market have asked for,** if you have notes: payment methods, invoices, language, data hosting.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected** with Search Console, GA4, Google Ads, Meta Ads and DataForSEO. The skill then reads demand by country, local search volumes and CPCs, and rivals' ads in that country itself. Without it, paste your numbers or exports and it works from those.

## Example requests

**A full entry plan**
Hey Claude, I just added the "market-entry-plan" skill. We sell scheduling software in the US and want to enter Germany. Build the entry plan: route, what to translate, German prices, channels and a 90-day test under $12,000.

**Is the demand already there?**
Hey Claude, I just added the "market-entry-plan" skill. Before we spend anything on France, show me what France already sends us: search impressions, sign-ups and paying customers, and whether that counts as proven demand.

**Price for a new market**
Hey Claude, I just added the "market-entry-plan" skill. Here are our plans and three Dutch rivals' prices. Set our euro price list for the Netherlands and tell me which plan is out of line.

**Partner or local company?**
Hey Claude, I just added the "market-entry-plan" skill. Two Australian agencies want to resell us. Should we start with them, sell direct, or set up a local company?

## What you get

1. **A demand verdict** for the market (proven, latent or none) with the evidence behind it.
2. **Local search demand and cost:** volumes, CPCs and how many paid clicks the market can really supply.
3. **Who competes there:** local and foreign rivals, their ads in that country, and the directories worth listing on.
4. **The entry route** now, the route next with its trigger, and how far you are from needing a local company.
5. **What to localise,** each item marked before the test, before scaling or not needed.
6. **A local price list,** checked against rivals' comparable plans.
7. **The channel mix and launch order,** each channel tested against a cost-per-customer ceiling from your own numbers.
8. **A 90-day test** with a budget, stop rules for days 30, 60 and 90, and the metrics to read every week.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact change, it can put the plan into a new tab in a Google Sheet. It never creates campaigns, changes budgets or targeting, or publishes anything.

## Tips

- Give it the market's own search terms from a native speaker if you have one; it never relies on machine translation alone.
- Legal, tax and data-protection items come back as questions for an adviser, not answers.
- Rerun it at day 30 and day 60 with the test's numbers to apply the stop rules.
