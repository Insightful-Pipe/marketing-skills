# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "email-lifecycle-strategy" skill. Build our email and retention strategy: who is in each customer stage, what each stage gets today, what is missing, and what to build in the next 90 days.

## What you need

- **Nothing connected, at the least.** Describe the business, what you sell and how long one purchase lasts, and the emails you send today. An order export (customer id and order date per order) and your list of flows make the plan exact.
- **Better: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected to Claude,** with the accounts you use: Klaviyo or Mailchimp, Shopify or WooCommerce, GA4, Meta Ads and Google Ads. The skill reads your orders, flows, campaigns, segments, key events and audiences itself.
- **How many flows your team can build a month.** Without it, the plan assumes 2.

## Example requests

**Full strategy for a store**
Hey Claude, I just added the "email-lifecycle-strategy" skill. We run a Shopify store with Klaviyo. Work out our customer stages from our own orders and tell me which flows to build first.

**Customer retention strategy**
Hey Claude, I just added the "email-lifecycle-strategy" skill. Too many customers buy once and never come back. Give me a customer retention strategy with a number to track for each stage.

**Starting from zero**
Hey Claude, I just added the "email-lifecycle-strategy" skill. We're launching a candle shop next month with no list yet. What should our email marketing strategy be, and what do we need to track from day one?

**Software or services**
Hey Claude, I just added the "email-lifecycle-strategy" skill. We sell monthly software plans. Map our lifecycle stages from sign-up to cancelled, and show where email, in-app messages, push and retargeting ads should take over.

## What you get

1. **The return window:** how long your customers usually take to buy again, measured from your orders.
2. **Five stages with rules you can measure:** subscriber, first-time buyer, repeat, at risk and lapsed, with how many people are in each now and how many enter each month.
3. **One number to move per stage,** with its baseline from your own data.
4. **A coverage map:** each stage's jobs by email, SMS, push and paid ads, marked covered, partial, not live, gap, needs setup or not applicable.
5. **A 90-day build order,** ranked by the people each build reaches a month and fitted to your team's capacity.
6. **The broadcast rhythm in outline:** how often each group hears from you and which kinds of campaigns lead.
7. **Measurement fixes** for anything the plan can't read yet.

## What it changes

Nothing. It reads and plans. Writing the flows, the dated campaign calendar and the paid audiences are handed to other skills, if you have them installed.

## Tips

- Run it once a quarter. The return window moves as your products and customers change.
- Give it at least 12 months of orders. With under 50 reorders, it assumes the return window from how long one purchase lasts, and says so.
- After the first builds go live, run it again to see the stage numbers move.
