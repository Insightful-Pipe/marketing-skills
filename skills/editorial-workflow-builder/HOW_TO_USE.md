# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "editorial-workflow-builder" skill. Design our content workflow from idea to published, and tell us how many new posts and refreshes we can really ship a month.

## What you need

- **Your team:** who works on content, their role and roughly how many hours a week each spends on it.
- **Your current steps,** in your own words: where ideas come from, who writes, edits, approves and publishes.
- **Hours per stage** for a new piece and for a refresh, even rough guesses. The skill labels guesses and tells you how to firm them up.
- **Optional, the InsightfulPipe MCP** with WordPress, Search Console, GA4 and Google Sheets. With them the skill measures your real output, how long drafts wait and what each kind of work earns. Without them, paste a list of your pieces with publish and last-updated dates, and your Search Console and GA4 page exports.

## Example requests

**Full workflow and capacity plan**
Hey Claude, I just added the "editorial-workflow-builder" skill. Our blog keeps slipping. Map how we work today, design a better content workflow with owners and turnaround times, and tell us how many posts we can publish a month.

**Approvals are the problem**
Hey Claude, I just added the "editorial-workflow-builder" skill. Every post waits a week for sign-off. Show me where drafts are stuck in WordPress and redesign who approves what.

**New posts or refreshes?**
Hey Claude, I just added the "editorial-workflow-builder" skill. Should we write new posts or update old ones? Use our Search Console and GA4 results to tell us which pays more per hour, and plan next month.

**From a pasted list**
Hey Claude, I just added the "editorial-workflow-builder" skill. Here is our post list with publish and update dates, and our team of four. Build an editorial workflow template and a status board for us.

## What you get

1. **Your current content production process, measured:** posts published each month, updates, what is waiting and for how long.
2. **New vs refresh:** clicks and key events per piece for new posts, refreshed posts and untouched older posts, and which pays more per hour.
3. **The workflow:** stages, one owner each and a backup where work waits too long, exit tests, turnaround targets, work-in-progress limits, sign-offs only where there is real risk, and where AI helps and where a person must check.
4. **A capacity plan:** new pieces and refreshes per month that fit everyone's hours, the bottleneck role, and what would raise output.
5. **Templates:** a brief, an editor's review and a sign-off note.
6. **A status board** with columns, a stage drop-down and your open drafts already in it.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact payload, it can create the status board as a new tab in a Google Sheet you choose, with the stage drop-down, filters and a frozen header. It never publishes, schedules, edits or deletes anything in WordPress.

## Tips

- Rerun it after six weeks of logging stage dates on the board: cycle time per stage becomes measured instead of unknown.
- If writers draft in Google Docs, say so; WordPress then shows only part of the work in progress.
- Time your next three pieces and give the real hours. The capacity plan is only as good as those numbers.
