# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "spokesperson-interview-prep" skill. Our CEO is on a podcast next week to talk about our industry. Research the host, give us three key messages, the likely questions with answers, and then run a mock interview.

## What you need

- **The interview details:** outlet or show, interviewer, format (print, podcast, live or recorded TV, radio, panel), length, date and topic.
- **What you'd rather not be asked:** complaints, price changes, outages, a rival's attack, anything off limits and why.
- **Figures you can say on record,** with their dates. Without them, the skill marks every figure it finds "check before saying".
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP.** With YouTube, DataForSEO or GA4 connected, Claude reads the show's recent episodes, the interviewer's news and blog coverage and your own website numbers itself. Without it, paste links or text of the interviewer's recent work.

## Example requests

**Podcast guest prep**
Hey Claude, I just added the "spokesperson-interview-prep" skill. I'm a guest on a small-business podcast on Tuesday. Here's the show's YouTube channel link. Prep me.

**Tough interview**
Hey Claude, I just added the "spokesperson-interview-prep" skill. A trade reporter wants to talk to our CEO about last month's outage and our price change. Give us the hostile questions and answers that stay honest.

**Live TV or radio**
Hey Claude, I just added the "spokesperson-interview-prep" skill. I have a four-minute live radio slot about our survey. Keep every answer short enough for live radio.

**Practice only**
Hey Claude, I just added the "spokesperson-interview-prep" skill. Here's my briefing doc. Play the interviewer, ask me the hardest questions one at a time and score my answers.

## What you get

1. **An interviewer profile:** recent pieces, a count of the topics they cover most, how they ask questions, and how they've covered your industry, with dates.
2. **Your goal and three key messages,** each with two proof points.
3. **A proof ledger:** every figure with its source, dates, what it really measures, and whether it's approved to say.
4. **15 to 20 likely questions,** at least four of them hostile, each with why it's likely, a risk level and an answer that answers first, then steers to a message, with a word count that fits the format.
5. **Lines to avoid,** with a better line for each.
6. **A one-page briefing sheet** for the morning of the interview.
7. **A mock interview in chat,** with each answer scored out of 5, any claim that isn't in your brief flagged, and a second, harder round on your weakest questions.

## What it can change for you

Nothing. It only reads your accounts and writes the brief.

## Tips

- Name the sore points up front. The hostile questions are only as good as what you tell it.
- Approve the figures before the interview, and say each one the way the ledger says it.
- Practise out loud. A typed answer that fits the word count can still run long when spoken.
