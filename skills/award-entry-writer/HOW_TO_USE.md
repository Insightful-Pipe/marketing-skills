# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. Find the marketing awards worth entering for our win-back email program, then write the entry for the best one.

## What you need

- **The work you want to enter:** what it was, when it started, and whether it's still running.
- **The objective you set before launch,** with the date it was written down. If there wasn't one, say so; the skill won't invent one.
- **The awards,** with links to their entry pages or the entry kits, or ask it to find them for your field and year.
- **Your budget per entry, how many entries you can write,** and any holidays before the deadlines.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP connected to Claude** with Klaviyo, GA4, Shopify, Google Ads or Meta. The skill then pulls the results itself for dated windows. Without it, paste the figures or an export.

## Example requests

**Find the awards worth entering**
Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. Which business awards should we enter this year? We're a B2B software company in Austin, our budget is $600 an entry, and we can write three entries.

**Write the entry for one award**
Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. Here's the entry kit for the award we're entering and our campaign notes. Write the award submission to their criteria and word limits.

**Build the results from our data**
Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. Pull the results for our spring Google Ads and Meta campaign from March to June against the period before and the same months last year, and tell me which numbers we can put in an award entry.

**Check a draft before it goes in**
Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. Here's our draft award application. Check every claim, score it the way a judge would, and tell me what to fix before the deadline.

**Enter a client's work**
Hey Claude, I just added the "award-entry-writer" skill. We're an agency entering a client's email program for an award. The client has agreed. Write the entry and list every attachment and sign-off we need, with dates.

## What you get

1. **A shortlist** of awards, each read from its own page and scored out of 10 on fit, proof, standing, and cost and effort, plus the awards it left out and why: deadline passed, work outside the eligibility window, or a trophy tied to buying a table.
2. **A dated calendar** for each award, worked back from the deadline: results frozen, first draft, sign-offs, submitted, then the shortlist and the ceremony.
3. **A criteria map** with a word budget for each criterion, split by its judging weight.
4. **The results,** for equal windows before and a year earlier, checked against the objective, the rest of the business and the season.
5. **A verdict on every claim** in your notes, and the wording the entry uses.
6. **The entry,** section by section, ready to paste, with word counts against each budget.
7. **A judge's read-through** of the draft, the attachments checklist and the data notes behind every number.

## What it can change for you

With your yes on the exact rows, it can write the awards calendar to a new tab in Google Sheets. It never submits an entry, pays a fee or books a table.

## Tips

- Give it the objective you wrote before launch. Judges weigh results against what you set out to do.
- Enter at the early deadline when you can; the calendar shows whether you'll make it.
- If you're entering the same work for two awards, ask for a second entry. It reuses the checked numbers and rebuilds the text for the second award's criteria.
