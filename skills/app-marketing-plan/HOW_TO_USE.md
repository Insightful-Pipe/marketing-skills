# How to Use This Skill

Hey Claude, I just added the "app-marketing-plan" skill. We launch our app on iOS and Android in March. Build the marketing plan from now to three months after launch.

## What you need

- **A one-line description of the app,** its platforms, and the launch date (or the date it went live).
- **Your prices** (monthly, yearly or one-time). The skill won't invent them, because the cost-per-install ceiling depends on them.
- **Your best guesses,** if you have them: the share of installs that will pay, how many monthly payers renew, and the retention you expect. Each guess is labelled "assumed" and checked in the soft launch.
- **Your paid budget per month,** if any. Without one, the plan shows the budget its targets would need.
- **Optional: the InsightfulPipe MCP.** With it, the skill reads App Store and Google Play search results itself, pulls web search volume for the web-to-app pages, and, once the app is live, reads its GA4 app data and its Google Ads, Meta, TikTok and Snapchat app-install campaigns. Without it, paste the top apps for your search terms and any exports you have.

## Example requests

**How to market an app before launch**
Hey Claude, I just added the "app-marketing-plan" skill. How do I market a habit tracker that launches on iOS on 6 April? We have $2,000 a month for ads and no audience yet.

**Increase app downloads for a live app**
Hey Claude, I just added the "app-marketing-plan" skill. Our Android app has been live for four months and downloads are flat. Read our GA4 and Google Ads data and plan the next three months.

**Pre-registration and soft launch**
Hey Claude, I just added the "app-marketing-plan" skill. Plan our Google Play pre-registration, App Store pre-order and a soft launch in Australia before a US launch on 1 June.

**What can we pay per install**
Hey Claude, I just added the "app-marketing-plan" skill. Our subscription is $6.99 a month. Work out the most we can pay per install and which ad channels to test first.

## What you get

1. **A category snapshot:** for each store search term, how many ratings page-one apps have, how many are stale or low-rated, the term to position on, the store category and a one-line positioning.
2. **What an install is worth:** the break-even and planning cost per install, with the math and a sensitivity line.
3. **A timeline dated back from launch day,** with the fixed store dates: when Google Play pre-registration opens and closes, when the App Store pre-order opens, the soft-launch gate and the build deadline.
4. **Eight workstreams** (positioning, ASO, pre-registration and pre-order, soft launch, paid user acquisition, creators, referral, web-to-app), each with dates, a target and an owner.
5. **Monthly targets:** paid and organic installs, new payers and retention targets, month by month.
6. **The soft-launch gate:** the checks that decide whether the launch spends money, and what changes if one fails.

## What it can change for you

Nothing. It reads public store data and, for a live app, your analytics and ad accounts, and writes the plan. Campaigns, store listings and pre-registration are set up by you or by the matching skill.

## Tips

- Give it the store search terms you'd type yourself, and say which ones describe what the app does.
- Run it again after the soft launch with the measured retention, payer rate and cost per install: the ceiling and the targets update.
- Apple Ads, AppLovin and your attribution tool have no connector; export spend, installs and day 7 retention from them and paste the numbers.
